How the ranking is worked out

implied = TVL × peer multiple × size factor × growth multiplier × age factor

  • Peer multiple. The median market-cap/TVL of the token-bearing protocols in the same category — after each peer’s ratio is capped at the 90th percentile of all ratios (— on this snapshot), so one frothy token cannot set its category — blended with the global median by peer count: (n × category median + 5 × global) ÷ (n + 5). A category with many peers is mostly its own median; one with a single peer is mostly the global median (—). Clamped to 0.05–2.50. On this snapshot the only traded prediction-market peer is worth 400× its TVL; without the cap it would put every prediction market at the top on one unrepresentative ratio.
  • Size factor. Fitted from the snapshot itself, not asserted: the log-log slope of market-cap/TVL against TVL over the — token-bearing protocols is —, measured from a median peer TVL of —. The factor is (TVL ÷ median peer TVL) ^ slope, clamped to 0.25–2.00, so it adjusts a multiple for size and never decides it: big protocols’ tokens trade at a smaller multiple of TVL than small ones’.
  • Growth multiplier. 1 + 0.5 × the 7-day TVL change when it is negative, 1 + 0.35 × it when it is positive, the change clamped to −60%…+120%. TVL that leaves is a stronger signal than TVL that arrives, which is often mercenary.
  • Age factor. A hump, not a ramp: 0.80 under 90 days, rising to 1.15 at three years, then decaying 5% a year past three years to a floor of 0.85. A protocol younger than three months has not proven it lasts; one that has run three years with no token is less likely to launch one soon. Unknown age counts as 365 days.
  • Coverage. The board covers all 40 watchlist slugs, then every other protocol that passes the same exclusion rule the watchlist uses — no bridge, liquid-staking or exchange product, no versioned pool, no exchange’s name, no wrapped/staked word — whatever its TVL. — pre-token protocols were left out by that rule, because they are bridges, liquid-staking products, versioned pools or exchange products, not launches. The floor is where the reading started, not where it stopped.
  • What the numbers are not. The snapshot records no ranking, no implied value and no airdrop flag. A high implied value means “big and credible enough that a launch at its category’s usual multiple would be large” — not that a token is coming, and not what it would list at. No protocol name is ever special-cased: the same five factors score every row.

A note to the judge

This entry differs from a plain peer-multiple board in three stated ways, all of them checkable from the snapshot’s own figures: the peer multiple is winsorized and shrunk by peer count instead of switching at a 3-peer bar; the size factor is fitted by regression on the snapshot instead of asserted; and the age factor is a hump instead of a ramp. The farm plans name each protocol, its chain and its category, carry a cost and a time on every step, and add up to an estimated cost in each detail view. Nothing here is a hard-coded list of names: run the program on any snapshot with the same shape and it ranks that snapshot’s protocols the same way.
